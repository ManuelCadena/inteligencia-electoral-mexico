Part I

The Mexican electoral system — institutional context for interpreting the data

Without this part, SHARE_TOTAL, GEO_KEY_2 or a coalition string like PAN_PRI_PRD are just tokens. This section translates the legal-territorial architecture into executable analysis rules. Base source: docs/KB_SISTEMA_ELECTORAL_MEXICO.md. CPEUMINE

I.1Institutions and official sources

Mexico operates a dual electoral federalism. The Instituto Nacional Electoral (INE) — the national electoral management body created in its current form by the 2014 reform that succeeded the Instituto Federal Electoral (IFE) — organizes federal contests: President, Federal Deputies and Senators. The 32 Organismos Públicos Locales Electorales (OPLE) — one local electoral institute per state — organize gubernatorial, local-deputy and municipal races. The Tribunal Electoral del Poder Judicial de la Federación (TEPJF) and its regional chambers adjudicate challenges — which matters analytically because annulled elections (e.g., several Chiapas municipalities in 2015) appear in the source with zero votes and must not be confused with abstention. K

Three result sources with distinct legal status: PREP (Programa de Resultados Electorales Preliminares — preliminary, unofficial, election-night count), the distrital computations (legally official tallies), and SICEE (Sistema de Consulta de la Estadística de las Elecciones — INE's consolidated historical statistics). Our dataset is built from SICEE: definitive, but containing source defects the pipeline flags with FLAG_CONSISTENCIA rather than silently correcting. KD

I.2Electoral geography — the territorial nesting chain

The atomic unit is the electoral section (~68–71 thousand nationwide, each covering 100–3,000 voters), which contains polling stations (casillas, ~170 thousand in 2024). Sections aggregate upward into local single-member districts (699 across all 32 states), federal single-member districts (300, per articles 52–53 of the Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos, CPEUM), municipalities (~2,469 plus 16 Mexico City alcaldías), and entities (32 states). For the Senate no federal district exists: senators are elected at the entity level. K

I.3Seat-allocation formulas — why vote share is not seat share

Two principles coexist. Mayoría Relativa (MR) — single-member plurality: whoever gets the most votes in the district wins the seat. Representación Proporcional (RP) — proportional representation: seats allocated from party lists by quotient and largest-remainder formulas, subject to constitutional caps (no party may exceed 300 federal deputies; an 8-point overrepresentation cap limits the gap between national vote share and seat share). The Senate adds a third device: primera minoría (first minority) — one seat per entity for the runner-up slate. K

I.4Parties and coalitions — the most fragile semantic field

PARTIDO_COALICION takes values like PAN, MORENA or PAN_PRI_PRD. The acronyms: PAN = Partido Acción Nacional (center-right, founded 1939); PRI = Partido Revolucionario Institucional (the hegemonic party 1929–2000); PRD = Partido de la Revolución Democrática (left, 1989); MORENA = Movimiento Regeneración Nacional (registered 2014); PT = Partido del Trabajo; PVEM = Partido Verde Ecologista; MC = Movimiento Ciudadano. A coalition is a joint candidacy, not a party: summing PAN + PRI + PAN_PRI_PRD rows in the same election can double-count when the source reports the coalition and its components separately. K Analysis rule: choose explicitly whether the coalition is treated as a single actor or redistributed under OPLE/INE rules, and state the choice in the notebook. The canonical dataset preserves source values; the consistency flag marks groups whose share sums fall outside [0.9, 1.1]. D

Critical acronym chronology: recent party extinctions — PANAL (Partido Nueva Alianza), PES (Partido Encuentro Social), RSP (Redes Sociales Progresistas), FXM (Fuerza por México), all de-registered after 2021 — and the rise of MORENA. A longitudinal model cannot treat the party vocabulary as stationary: it needs an equivalence table or per-cycle learned embeddings (see IV.5). K

I.5Historical electoral map 1988–2024

  • PRI hegemony (–1988): party-state regime; effectively competitive elections begin with the 1988 cardenista rupture. Pre-1991 SICEE data show low real competitiveness — useful as a baseline, dangerous as a signal. K
  • Transition (1988–2000): alternation in Baja California 1989 (first opposition governor), presidential alternation 2000 (PAN). K
  • North–south polarization (2006–2012): PAN strong in the industrial north; PRD in Mexico City and the south; PRI territorially resilient. KL
  • MORENA nationalization (2018–2024): historically unprecedented territorial sweep — in 2018 MORENA wins entities that had never voted left; 2024 widens the map. For modeling: a regime change that invalidates pre-2018 regularities unless temporal weighting or per-regime parameters are used. K

I.7Temporal-comparability warnings

Non-comparability checklist
  • Federal redistricting 2005/2017/2022: federal ID_DISTRITO is not stable across processes — a panel analysis by district that crosses a redistricting must bridge via section or municipality, or treat the district as a new unit. K
  • DF→CDMX (2016): the pipeline maps Distrito Federal to CIUDAD DE MEXICO; alcaldías comparable to municipalities from 2018. D
  • Usos y costumbres: ~418 of 570 Oaxaca municipalities select authorities without partisan elections — producing structural zeros, not abstention. Model separately or exclude. K
  • Re-election: municipal re-election exists only from ~2018; federal legislative re-election from 2021 — incumbency variables are censored by nature, not by missing data. K
  • Staggered gubernatorial calendars: state cycles do not coincide; in 2015–2024 each entity has its own calendar — ANIO alone does not define the panel; the unit is (office × entity). K
