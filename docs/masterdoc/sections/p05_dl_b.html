III.7 Transformers — attention over electoral history

Self-attention, positional encoding, encoder/decoder, context without recurrence.

Why it exists

A recurrent network must "pass through" 2006 to influence 2024; attention accesses any past cycle directly. For one entity, the question "how much does the 2018 anomaly weigh versus the 1991–2017 regime?" is literally an attention distribution — the mechanism is simultaneously a predictor and an interpretability instrument.

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 7 — Transformers. Read that short segment first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

Scaled dot-product self-attention (Vaswani et al. 2017): each position emits a query Q, key K and value V; the output is a weighted sum of values where weights measure query–key compatibility, scaled by √dk to keep softmax gradients stable. Multi-head attention (MHA) runs h parallel attention functions on different projections — different heads can attend to long regime, recent shock and sexenio cycle simultaneously. Positional encoding injects order (sinusoidal or learned); with irregular local calendars we use a continuous ANIO embedding. Causal masking blocks attention to future positions — attending to the future is leakage by definition. L

Attn(Q,K,V) = softmax( QK⊤ / √dk ) · V Q,K,V = projections of the unit's electoral-cycle sequence

A Transformer block stacks: LayerNorm (normalizes activations per position) → MHA → residual connection → position-wise feed-forward (two dense layers, GELU) → residual. Complexity O(T2·d) in sequence length — the reason state-space alternatives exist (III.14). L

Architecture

Dataset binding

Unit: entity (32 long sequences) and municipality (short irregular sequences). Per-token inputs: SHARE_VALIDO per party + macro_lag + structurals + temporal position. Target: next-cycle shares. Attention maps are reported as a paper figure — treated as evidence, not postulate. P

III.8 Speech processing — the ingestion stage feeding NLP

Spectrograms, Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), automatic transcription.

Why it exists

The mañaneras are audio/video. Each conference (~1–2 h daily, 2018–) becomes text through ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition — e.g., Whisper). This week is the data-ingestion stage of the textual channel: not an electoral model, but the factory producing the corpus analyzed in Part IV. P

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 8 — automatic speech recognition. Read that short segment first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

A waveform is transformed into a Mel spectrogram S(t,f): a time–frequency map on the perceptually motivated Mel scale. An ASR encoder-decoder maps S to a token distribution P(tokens | S); decoding (beam search) yields the transcript, and forced alignment or timestamped decoding assigns each segment a time interval. Quality is measured by WER (Word Error Rate = (substitutions+insertions+deletions)/N words). L

signal → Mel spectrogram S(t,f) → ASR encoder → P(tokens | S) timestamped decoding → temporal index of mentions

Dataset binding

The mañaneras corpus is already assembled from structured transcripts (NOSTRODATA + roicort, 514,909 interventions) — for the ~2 years where we have audio only, or as validation, ASR supplies timestamps and confidences. Output: (fecha, segment, text, asr_confidence) rows into corpus_mananeras. Anti-leakage: the conference's publication date governs availability. D

III.9 Seq2Seq and LSTM/GRU — the memory of the electoral cycle

Encoder-decoder, LSTM/GRU gates, teacher forcing, sequence forecasting.

Why it exists

The vote has structural memory: incumbent punishment, stronghold inertia, the sexenio effect. A recurrent network over each unit's cycle sequence is the honest neural temporal baseline — expressive enough for the dynamics, small enough for the available n.

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 9 — LSTM/GRU and seq2seq. Read that short segment first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

An RNN (Recurrent Neural Network) maintains hidden state ht = f(ht−1, xt); plain RNNs suffer vanishing gradients over long spans. LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory, Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997) adds a cell state ct and three gates — input i, forget f, output o — that learn what to write, keep and emit: ct = f⊙ct−1 + i⊙tanh(c̃). GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit, Cho et al. 2014) merges the gates (update z, reset r) for fewer parameters. Seq2seq (encoder–decoder) maps an input sequence to a context vector hT, then decodes the target autoregressively; teacher forcing feeds the true previous output during training. L

ct = ft⊙ct−1 + it⊙c̃t , ht = ot⊙tanh(ct) the forget gate decides what the system "remembers" — regime vs anomaly

Dataset binding

Same sequence format as III.7 — the LSTM-vs-Transformer comparison on the identical split is itself an experiment, not a detail. Bidirectionality is forbidden (it would violate temporal causality). Multi-step: the decoder emits the full next-cycle share vector (compositional). P

III.10 LLMs · III.11 RAG · III.12 Agents — the knowledge layer

Large Language Models, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, agentic workflows.

Why it exists

These three weeks do not train on the dataset: they form the interpretation layer. The Part-I KB (353 lines of electoral rules) is exactly the knowledge a model cannot memorize reliably: where a district is not comparable, what PAN_PRI_PRD means, why a zero in Oaxaca is not abstention. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) connects the language model to that knowledge; agents orchestrate the analysis reproducibly. P

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 10, Topic 11, Topic 12 — LLMs, RAG and agentic workflows. Read those short segments first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

LLM: a Transformer language model P(next token | prefix) pre-trained at scale; its parameters encode statistical regularities but not our private KB. RAG (Lewis et al. 2020): retrieve the k most relevant document chunks by dense embedding similarity, condition generation on them — hallucination is bounded by what was retrieved. Agents: an LLM in a tool-use loop (plan → call tool → observe → revise) with an audit trail of every action. L

P(answer | q) = Σd∈top-k P(d | q) · P(answer | q, d) dense retriever (BGE-M3/BETO embeddings) over KB + methodology chunks

Dataset binding

  • Base LLM (W10): generates interpretations of results under a hard gate — every claim must cite a data row or a KB section; detected hallucination = error.
  • RAG (W11): vector index over KB_SISTEMA_ELECTORAL_MEXICO.md + METODOLOGIA.md + data dictionary; queries like "is DIPUTADO_LOCAL_MR comparable between 2015 and 2021?" answered with citations.
  • Analyst agent (W12): tool-orchestrated loop: load cohort → run QA → emit mini-report with a seal of consulted data. Full audit trail (which file, which query, which result). P

III.13 Generative models · III.14 Transformer replacements

GANs/diffusion as simulation; State-Space Models (Mamba, TSMixer) as efficient alternatives.

Why it exists

Generative: simulating counterfactual scenarios ("what would 2024 look like with 2018 inflation?") — pedagogically valuable, scientifically fragile: reported as illustrative, never as findings. State-Space Models (SSMs) such as Mamba (Gu & Dao 2023) or TSMixer replace quadratic attention with linear-time recurrence — for our short-T panel the gain is efficiency, not capacity; the ablation documents whether the complexity is worth it.

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 13, Topic 14 — generative and state-space models. Read those short segments first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

GAN (Generative Adversarial Network, Goodfellow et al. 2014): a generator G and discriminator D play a minimax game — G synthesizes, D detects; equilibrium yields samples approximating pdata. SSM: a continuous linear dynamical system ht = A·ht−1 + B·xt, yt = C·ht — a recurrence linear in the state, hence O(T) instead of attention's O(T2); Mamba makes A,B,C input-dependent ("selective"), recovering content-aware behavior. L

SSM: ht = A ht−1 + B xt , yt = C ht  |  selective: A,B,C depend on xt linear-in-state recurrence → O(T) vs attention's O(T2)

Dataset binding

Generative (A9, ILLUSTRATIVE): the VAE decoder (III.6) perturbed by synthetic macro generates counterfactual municipal electoral profiles — a sensitivity figure, explicitly labeled pedagogical. SSM (A10): TSMixer or Mamba-lite on the same III.7/III.9 sequences — direct MAE vs training-cost comparison. The expected conclusion is a design datum: with T≈5–10 cycles, does SSM efficiency beat attention's interpretability? P
