IV.1 Preprocessing and BoW/TF-IDF — the cheapest textual baseline · IV.2 Text RNN

Normalization, stopwords, classical vectorization; recurrent networks over token sequences.

Why it exists

Before BERT we must know how much simple lexical signal exists: does the frequency of "seguridad" (security), "inflación", "empleo" in the weeks before an election correlate with the outcome? Bag-of-Words (BoW) plus TF-IDF is the textual baseline: cheap, transparent, interpretable — and hard to beat substantially if the signal is shallow.

Theoretical framework

BoW: a document becomes a vector of token counts — order is discarded. TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency) reweights by rarity: tokens frequent in one document but rare across the corpus get high weight — TF-IDF(w,d) = TF(w,d)·log(N/df_w) where df_w is the number of documents containing w. Text RNN: an LSTM processes tokens in order — it captures "no queremos" vs "queremos" (negation scope) that BoW loses. L

TF-IDF(w,d) = TF(w,d) · log( N / dfw ) classifier: ŷ = softmax(W·tfidf(d)) per pre-electoral window

Dataset binding

90-day pre-election window per entity (locals) or nationally (federals); features = weekly averaged TF-IDF vectors. Targets: incumbent SHARE_VALIDO and ALTERNANCIA. This baseline sets the textual floor: everything after it must improve on it or justify its complexity. RNN variant: LSTM 1×128 over each discourse's token sequence; direct comparison with IV.1 quantifies the value of lexical order in this domain. P

IV.3 Tokenization · IV.4 N-grams — the morphology of political discourse

BPE/subword units, domain tokenizer; n-gram language models.

Why it exists

Mexican political Spanish has its own morphology: "chairos", "fifís", "mañanera", acronyms (INE, MORENA, IMSS) behaving as atomic tokens. A generic tokenizer splits these units; training BPE (Byte-Pair Encoding) on the corpus quantifies the problem (out-of-vocabulary rate) and yields a domain tokenizer. P

Theoretical framework

BPE (Gage 1994; Sennrich et al. 2016): start from characters, greedily merge the most frequent adjacent pair until the vocabulary reaches size B — frequent words become single tokens, rare words decompose into subwords, so OOV (out-of-vocabulary) vanishes by construction. N-gram LM: the Markovian model P(w_t | w_{t−n+1..t−1}) estimated by counts with smoothing; perplexity = exp(−(1/N)Σ log P) is the effective vocabulary branching — lower = more predictable text. L

BPE: greedy merges of most-frequent pairs · vocab B · OOV-rate = |unknown| / |tokens| domain tokenizer vs pre-trained BETO tokenizer
n-gram LM: P(wt | wt−n+1..t−1) · Perplexity = exp(−(1/N)Σ log P)

Dataset binding

N-grams per sexenio (six-year term): cross-perplexity — how predictable is 2024 discourse from a model trained on 2018? The sexenio distance in n-gram space is a quantitative proxy of the rhetorical regime change documented qualitatively in §I.5. Derived feature: drift_retorico_sexenio. P

IV.5 Word embeddings · IV.6 Dimensionality reduction and visualization

Word2Vec/GloVe, semantic similarity; PCA/t-SNE/UMAP for inspection.

Why it exists

Embeddings turn the intuition "MORENA is the PRD's successor" into measurable geometry: if the post-2018 MORENA embedding sits closer to pre-2018 PRD than to its own history, semantic drift is quantifiable. And visualization is the pedagogical layer: a semantic map of the political lexicon per era communicates more than a thousand tables.

Theoretical framework

Word2Vec (Mikolov et al. 2013): skip-gram learns vectors by predicting context — words sharing contexts land close in R^d; the geometry supports analogies vrey − vhombre + vmujer ≈ vreina. GloVe (Pennington et al. 2014) factorizes the log co-occurrence matrix instead. t-SNE (van der Maaten & Hinton 2008): embeds high-D into 2-D by minimizing the KL divergence between neighborhood similarities — local structure is faithful, global distances are not interpretable. UMAP is a faster, better-preserving alternative. L

skip-gram: max Σ log P(context | w) ; analogy: va−vb+vc ≈ vd t-SNE minimizes KL(high-D ‖ 2-D) — do not read global distances

Dataset binding

Two uses: (a) word embeddings trained on mañaneras → t-SNE map of the political lexicon per sexenio, showing the migration of "pobreza", "corrupción", "transformación"; (b) entity and party embeddings learned jointly with the MLP (III.1) — does Oaxaca's embedding sit near Chiapas's? The artifact is double-valued: a feature (model) and a figure (paper). P
