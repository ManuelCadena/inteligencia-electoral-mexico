IV.7 Topic modeling (LDA) — the presidential agenda as a variable

Latent Dirichlet Allocation, topic coherence, thematic prevalence.

Why it exists

"What the government talks about" is a state variable with a political-science pedigree (agenda-setting). Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA, Blei, Ng & Jordan 2003) decomposes the corpus into K topics and, per day/week, a prevalence distribution: the series share_topic_seguridad becomes a structural covariate, not a metadata tag.

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 21 — LDA topic modeling. Read that short segment first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

LDA is a generative probabilistic model: each document d draws a topic-mixture θ_d ~ Dir(α); each topic k is a word distribution φ_k ~ Dir(β); each observed word picks a topic z then a word from φ_z. Inference (variational Bayes or Gibbs sampling) recovers θ and φ; the Dirichlet prior encourages sparse, interpretable mixtures. Coherence (C_v) measures how semantically consistent a topic's top words are — used to select K. L

P(w) = Σk P(w|z=k) · P(z=k|d) ,  θd ~ Dir(α) , φk ~ Dir(β) θ_d = topic prevalence in document d; temporal aggregate → feature

Dataset binding

Mañaneras segmented by day → LDA K≈20–30 → weekly prevalence per topic → joined to elections by date (last N pre-election weeks). Measurable hypothesis: pre-vote prevalence of "seguridad"/"economía" correlates with outcomes — and crucially, LDA provides the interpretable baseline the BETO branch (IV.12) must beat. P

IV.8 Structural Topic Models — topics conditioned on context

STM: prevalence and content as functions of document covariates.

Why it exists

LDA ignores that a 2019 discourse is not the same context as a 2023 one. The Structural Topic Model (STM, Roberts, Stewart & Airoldi 2016) makes thematic prevalence a function of covariates: sexenio year, pre-electoral quarter, current inflation. Natural question: "does the share of security discourse rise when ENSU perception rises?" — that is a statistical model on text, not mere clustering. P

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 22 — structural topic models. Read that short segment first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

STM generalizes LDA: the topic-prevalence parameter is drawn from a logistic normal whose mean is a regression on document covariates Γ·x_d; topic content can also depend on covariates. Estimated Γ coefficients are directly interpretable — "one extra inflation point ↔ +Δ topic-economy share" is a substantive contribution, not just a feature. L

θd ~ LogisticNormal( Γ xd , Σ ) ,  φk covariate-dependent x_d = document covariates: week, economic indicator, pre-electoral dummy

Dataset binding

Covariates x_d from the canonical set: AÑO_SEXENIO, ES_INTERMEDIA, macro_lag at the document's date. The Γ table with confidence intervals is a paper deliverable. P

IV.9 Text classification · IV.10 NER — stance and territorial attention

Supervised document classification; Named Entity Recognition.

Why it exists

Two high-value electoral features: (a) discourse stance — is the tone toward opposition/economy/security positive, negative, combative? — and (b) presidential territorial attention — a weighted count of entity/municipality mentions in the discourse: does the president talk more about states where he loses? That last one is an original political covariate only the mañaneras corpus enables. P

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 23, Topic 24 — text classification/stance and NER. Read those short segments first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

Sequence labeling with NER (Named Entity Recognition): assign each token a BIO tag (Begin/Inside/Outside of an entity: PER person, ORG organization, LOC location, GPE geopolitical entity) maximizing P(y|x) over tag sequences. Text classification: a document vector → softmax over classes; fine-tuning a pre-trained encoder (IV.12) replaces the shallow BoW classifier. L

NER: argmaxy Π P(yt | x, yt−1) · BIO tags · entities: PER, ORG, LOC, GPEGPE/LOC mentions map to ENTIDAD_CANONICA and GEO_KEY_2

Dataset binding

Spanish fine-tuned NER (or BETO-NER) yields, per intervention, territorial entities normalized to the canonical key. Weekly aggregation per entity → feature atencion_presidencial_ent. Stance: fine-tuned classifier with our own labels (positive/negative/neutral/combat) annotated on a sample — inter-annotator agreement or LLM-assisted validation reported. P

IV.11 Sequential CRF · IV.12 Attention and BETO — from interpretable to encoder

Conditional Random Fields as sequence baseline; self-attention and Spanish encoders.

Why it exists

A Conditional Random Field (CRF) is the structured sequence-labeling baseline: its learned transition matrix is directly interpretable (which tags may follow which). BETO (dccuchile/bert-base-spanish-wwm-cased, a BERT — Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers — trained on Spanish text) is the domain encoder: its [CLS] token embedding (or mean pooling over tokens) is the textual vector feeding the multimodal fusion (Part V). The CRF-vs-transformer comparison on NER documents the concrete gain of deep learning on this task, not in the abstract. P

Theoretical framework

Need a gentler start? Part VIII explains the concepts behind this section from first principles (why they matter, how they work, and an everyday analogy) in Topic 25, Topic 26 — CRFs and BETO/BERT. Read those short segments first if any term below feels unfamiliar.

CRF (Lafferty et al. 2001): a discriminative undirected graphical model — P(y|x) ∝ exp(Σ_t [ s(y_t,x,t) + A_{y_{t−1},y_t} ]) — where s scores emission and A is the learned transition matrix; decoding by Viterbi. BERT: a bidirectional Transformer encoder pre-trained with Masked Language Modeling (predict masked tokens) and next-sentence objectives; fine-tuning adapts it to a task with a small head. L

CRF: P(y|x) ∝ exp( Σt [ s(yt,x,t) + Ayt−1,yt ] )  |  BERT pool: h = MeanPool(Encoder(tokens)) A = interpretable transition matrix · h ∈ R^768 = fusion input

Dataset binding

CRF: orthographic features (capitalization, prefixes, ±2 context) on the same NER data — interpretable baseline against BETO-NER (IV.10). BETO: fine-tune on stance classification (IV.9) plus the unfrozen encoder as an embedding extractor for fusion — report whether domain fine-tuning improves the generic pool. P
